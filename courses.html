AI in Healthcare

Learn what AI is, where it may be used, and why professional review, privacy, and responsible design are important.

Take the AI in Healthcare quiz →

Lesson 1: Understanding AI

Artificial intelligence is a broad term for computer systems that perform tasks such as recognizing patterns in information. Different AI systems are built for different tasks and can make mistakes.

Lesson 2: AI in healthcare

AI tools may support research, help organize information, or assist professionals with specific workflows. Their results need context and review from qualified people.

Lesson 3: Responsible use

Responsible use considers privacy, accuracy, fairness, and human oversight. AI should not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.

Knowledge check: What is a responsible way to use AI in healthcare?

Use AI as a support tool, protect private information, and have qualified professionals review its results.

Medical Technology

Explore how medical tools and devices are designed to support patients and healthcare professionals.

Lesson 1: What is medical technology?

Medical technology includes tools, devices, and software used in healthcare settings. Each tool is designed for a particular purpose.

Lesson 2: Designing for people

Good design considers safety, accessibility, ease of use, and the needs of the people who use the technology.

Lesson 3: Evaluating new tools

New technology should be carefully evaluated for its intended use, reliability, limitations, and effects on people.

Knowledge check: Name one important design consideration.

Safety, accessibility, and ease of use are all important considerations.

Digital Health

Learn about digital services that connect people, information, and healthcare teams.

Lesson 1: Telemedicine

Telemedicine uses digital communication tools to support remote interactions between patients and healthcare professionals.

Lesson 2: Health information and privacy

Health information is sensitive. Digital health services should handle it carefully and explain how it is used.

Lesson 3: Connected care

Digital tools can help connect services, but they should be designed to work for people with different needs and levels of access.

Knowledge check: Why should health information be protected?

It is sensitive personal information and should be handled with care and respect for privacy.
