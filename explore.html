A device reports an unexpected change

A connected health device records a change and sends an alert. Before drawing conclusions, explore what questions a team should ask.

1. What should we check about the data?

Consider whether the reading is complete, current, and collected under the expected conditions.

2. What should we ask about the technology?

Understand what the tool was designed to do, how reliable it is, and what limitations are known.

3. Why does human review matter?

Technology can provide information, but qualified people need to interpret it in context.

Evaluate a MedTech idea

Use these questions to examine a health technology idea from more than one angle.

1. What problem is it trying to solve?

Describe the need clearly. Who experiences it, and what happens today?

2. What evidence supports the idea?

Look for reliable evidence that matches the intended use and the people it is meant to serve.

3. What data does it need?

Consider data quality, privacy, security, and whether the information is appropriate for the task.

4. How will people review its results?

Define who checks the output, how errors are handled, and when human judgment is needed.

5. Is it usable and accessible?

Consider the needs, abilities, language, and working environment of the people using it.
